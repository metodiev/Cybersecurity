# Research Project Rubrics

> **Audience:** learner, advisor, and independent reviewer. **Scale:** 100 points per project. **Mastery:** 80 points, no criterion below 60%, no critical failure.
> These rubrics assess research quality, not prose polish alone. A claim without evidence cannot earn credit because it is well written.

## Scoring levels

| Level | Points | Descriptor |
|---|---:|---|
| Exemplary | 90–100% of criterion | Complete, precise, reproducible, and critically examined |
| Proficient | 80–89% | Correct and evidence-based with minor gaps |
| Passing | 70–79% | Mostly correct; some claims lack evidence or nuance |
| Not yet | below 70% | Missing evidence, incorrect reasoning, or unsupported claims |

## P1: Structured Literature Review

| # | Criterion | Weight | Passing | Proficient | Exemplary |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Question and scope | 10 | Question is stated | Population, outcome, and time frame are bounded | Scope is justified and feasible |
| 2 | Search protocol | 20 | Names databases and queries | Records dates, filters, and result counts | Another reviewer can rerun the search |
| 3 | Screening | 15 | Applies criteria | Records exclusion reasons | Uses a second screener or agreement check |
| 4 | Evidence extraction | 20 | Extracts claims | Separates claim, method, and evidence | Records uncertainty and conflicts |
| 5 | Synthesis | 15 | Groups themes | Compares agreements and contradictions | Assesses strength of evidence |
| 6 | Gap statement | 10 | Names a gap | Connects gap to evidence | Proposes a feasible study that addresses it |
| 7 | Limitations | 5 | Lists limitations | Discusses coverage and bias | Quantifies or bounds the likely impact |
| 8 | Integrity | 5 | No fabricated sources or counts | Labels preprints and grey literature | Distinguishes primary, secondary, and generated material |

**Critical failures:** fabricated search counts, citations not read, no protocol, or a gap that does not follow from the screened evidence.

## P2: Replication Study

| # | Criterion | Weight | Passing | Proficient | Exemplary |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Original claim and scope | 10 | States the claim | States population and conditions | Identifies ambiguity in the original |
| 2 | Pre-specified protocol | 20 | Describes the replication | Fixes the consistency criterion before running | Includes a deviations policy and sensitivity plan |
| 3 | Artifact review | 15 | Lists available artifacts | Identifies missing components | Assesses whether the artifact supports the claim |
| 4 | Execution and evidence | 20 | Runs the study | Records environment and commands | Another researcher can rerun it |
| 5 | Comparison and uncertainty | 15 | Compares results | Reports intervals or variance | Discusses what would change the conclusion |
| 6 | Deviations | 5 | Logs changes | Explains expected impact | Tests sensitivity to a key deviation |
| 7 | Limitations | 5 | Lists limits | Distinguishes reproduction from replication | States what the replication cannot establish |
| 8 | Integrity and safety | 10 | No fabricated output | Authorization or ethics documented | Disclosure and artifact restrictions are explicit |

**Critical failures:** changing the criterion after seeing results, fabricating output, unauthorized testing, or presenting the original claim as the replicator's result.

## P3: Controlled Experiment

| # | Criterion | Weight | Passing | Proficient | Exemplary |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Question and contribution | 10 | States a question | States a testable comparison | Contribution and scope are precise |
| 2 | Design | 20 | Names a design | Defines unit, treatment, baseline, controls | Justifies design against alternatives |
| 3 | Sampling and power | 10 | Describes a sample | Defines sampling frame and exclusions | Provides power or precision justification |
| 4 | Measurement | 15 | Defines metrics | Defines denominators and instruments | Accounts for cost asymmetry and validation |
| 5 | Analysis and uncertainty | 20 | Reports results | Reports intervals and effect sizes | Handles multiplicity and sensitivity |
| 6 | Alternative explanations | 10 | Names one alternative | Tests or addresses it | Rules out the main rival explanation |
| 7 | Ethics and reproducibility | 10 | Documents authorization and artifact | Environment and commands are sufficient | Independent rerun is plausible |
| 8 | Writing | 5 | Clear structure | Claims match evidence | Limitations and scope are explicit |

**Critical failures:** no baseline, test-set leakage, fabricated data, missing ethics or authorization, or a causal claim the design cannot support.

## P4: Thesis Proposal and Paper

| # | Criterion | Weight | Passing | Proficient | Exemplary |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Original contribution | 15 | States a contribution | Contribution is bounded and novel | Contribution changes the state of evidence |
| 2 | Literature and gap | 10 | Reviews relevant work | Synthesizes and identifies a gap | Gap is demonstrated, not asserted |
| 3 | Method | 20 | Describes a method | Method fits the question and is reproducible | Assumptions and alternatives are justified |
| 4 | Evidence and reproducibility | 20 | Presents evidence | Evidence is traceable and artifact documented | Independent rerun or principled restriction |
| 5 | Analysis and limitations | 15 | Reports results | Reports uncertainty and limitations | Alternative explanations are addressed |
| 6 | Ethics and disclosure | 10 | Documents approvals | Disclosure plan is explicit | Dual-use and open-science trade-offs are reasoned |
| 7 | Writing and defense | 10 | Understandable | Claims map to evidence | Oral defense handles counterarguments |

**Critical failures:** no original contribution, unverifiable artifact with no justified restriction, fabricated data or citations, ethics or authorization failure, or inability to explain a central claim without notes.

## Reviewer procedure

1. Read the deliverables, not only the summary.
2. Trace three randomly selected claims to evidence.
3. Check the artifact or the restriction statement.
4. Check the ethics and authorization documents.
5. Score each criterion independently.
6. Write two strengths, two required revisions, and one next action.
7. Record the total and any critical failure.
8. Return the review to the learner and, for P4, to the panel.

## Appeal and revision

A failed criterion may be revised and resubmitted once per review cycle. The reviewer must use the same rubric and record what changed. A critical failure involving fabrication, unauthorized testing, or missing ethics cannot be remediated by prose revision alone.
